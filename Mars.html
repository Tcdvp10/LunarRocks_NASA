Mars mission timeline

Decades of discovery

Follow the rover milestones and read each explorer’s story alongside what it helped us learn. The first-person stories are imagined; the mission facts are real.

Every rover here remains on Mars. Green marks an active mission; orange marks a mission that has ended.

  1. 1997Mission ended · still on Mars

    Sojourner explores Mars

    The first successful wheeled rover on Mars studied nearby rocks and soil, showing that a robot could explore the planet’s surface.

    I was small enough to fit into a lander, but my wheels carried me across another planet. I studied rocks one careful move at a time and helped make future Mars rovers possible.
    Sojourner’s imagined story Official NASA mission page ↗
  2. 2004Mission ended · still on Mars

    Spirit arrives at Gusev Crater

    Spirit explored rocks and soil for clues about how water changed Mars long ago.

    I crossed a crater, climbed hills and examined rocks shaped by water long before I arrived. My wheels eventually stopped moving, but the clues I found kept travelling back to Earth.
    Spirit’s imagined story Official NASA mission page ↗
  3. 2004Mission ended · still on Mars

    Opportunity begins a long journey

    Spirit’s twin travelled across Mars and found strong clues that water once flowed across parts of the planet.

    I was designed for a short journey, but I kept going. I crossed plains, climbed craters and sent home evidence that Mars was once a very different place.
    Opportunity’s imagined story Official NASA mission page ↗
  4. 2012Active mission · still on Mars

    Curiosity lands in Gale Crater

    The rover studies Martian rocks to learn whether ancient Mars had places where tiny living things could have survived.

    I am a science lab on wheels. I drill into rocks and climb Mount Sharp to learn how Mars changed over time.
    Curiosity’s imagined story Official NASA mission page ↗
  5. 2021Active mission · still on Mars

    Perseverance begins a new chapter

    Exploring Jezero Crater’s ancient river delta, the rover searches for signs of past life and collects rocks for possible study on Earth.

    I arrived with a tiny helicopter tucked beneath me and a plan to explore an ancient lakebed. I collect carefully selected rocks because one day, scientists hope to study them in laboratories on Earth.
